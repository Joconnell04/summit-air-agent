# Heating and cooling basics

## Heating systems in New York City homes

Most homes in New York City are heated by a boiler or a furnace. A boiler heats water, or turns it into steam, that runs through pipes to radiators or baseboard heaters in each room. A furnace heats air and blows it through ducts and vents. Older apartment buildings usually have steam heat; houses and newer buildings more often have hot-water boilers or furnaces. Many boilers and furnaces burn natural gas or oil.

A heat pump moves heat instead of making it: in winter it pulls heat in from outside air, and in summer it runs in reverse to cool. A ductless mini-split is a kind of heat pump with an outdoor unit and one or more wall or ceiling units inside, each heating and cooling its own room.

## Cooling systems

Central air uses an outdoor unit (the condenser) and an indoor coil, usually on top of the furnace, and cools the whole home through the same ducts the furnace uses. Mini-splits cool room by room. Window units sit in a window and cool one room.

## Common sounds and symptoms, in plain words

- Banging, knocking or clanking pipes are common with steam heat. They often come from water trapped in the pipes, which a technician can check.
- Hissing from a radiator valve is normal for steam heat in small amounts.
- One cold radiator while the rest are warm usually points to that radiator or its valve, not the whole system.
- A furnace that turns on and off every few minutes is called short cycling. It can come from a dirty filter, a thermostat in the wrong spot, or an oversized unit.
- Air that blows but isn't warm, or isn't cold, can come from the thermostat setting, a dirty filter, low refrigerant on an air conditioner, or a problem with the outdoor unit.
- Water around a boiler, a furnace or an indoor AC unit is a leak. With air conditioning it's often a clogged drain line that carries away condensation.
- Ice on the outdoor unit or the copper lines of an air conditioner usually means poor airflow or low refrigerant.

## Filters and thermostats

Furnace and central-air systems have an air filter, usually where the return duct meets the unit. A clogged filter cuts airflow, makes the system work harder and can make rooms uneven. Many filters are changed every one to three months.

A thermostat tells the system when to run. Programmable and smart thermostats change the temperature on a schedule. A blank thermostat screen often means dead batteries or a tripped breaker.

## Maintenance and tune-ups

A tune-up is a yearly check and cleaning of equipment that still works. For heating it's best done in the fall, before the cold; for cooling, in the spring. A technician typically inspects and cleans burners, checks safety controls and the flue on gas equipment, tests the thermostat, checks electrical connections, and on air conditioners cleans the coils and checks refrigerant. Regular tune-ups help equipment last longer and catch small problems early.

## Equipment age

Many boilers last 15 to 30 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and central air conditioners and heat pumps 10 to 15, depending on care and use.

## Carbon monoxide

Carbon monoxide is a gas with no color or smell, made when fuel burns. Equipment that burns gas or oil, like boilers, furnaces and water heaters, needs working vents. New York City requires carbon monoxide alarms in homes.
