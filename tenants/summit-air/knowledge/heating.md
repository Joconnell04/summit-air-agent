# Heating equipment

## Gas furnaces

A furnace burns gas or oil to heat air, and a blower pushes that warm air through ducts to vents in each room. Furnaces are more common in houses than in older apartment buildings. Common brands in the New York area include Carrier, Trane, Lennox, Goodman and Rheem.

A furnace has a flue that carries exhaust outside. Gas equipment needs a working vent, which is one reason yearly tune-ups check the flue.

## Steam boilers and radiators

Most older apartment buildings and many older houses in New York City are heated by a steam boiler. The boiler boils water, and the steam rises through pipes to cast-iron radiators. Each radiator has a small air vent that lets air out so steam can fill it; the vent closes once steam reaches it. When the radiator cools, the steam turns back into water that drains back to the boiler.

A steam boiler usually has a glass tube on the side that shows the water level. Common brands include Burnham, Weil-McLain, Peerless, Slant/Fin and Utica.

- Banging, knocking or clanking in steam pipes usually means water is pooled somewhere in a pipe or radiator and the steam is pushing through it. A radiator that has shifted so it no longer tilts back toward its pipe is a common reason.
- A steam radiator that stays cold while others heat up often has a stuck or worn air vent, or a valve that is partly closed. On a one-pipe steam radiator the valve is meant to be fully open or fully closed.
- Hissing from a radiator vent while it fills with steam is normal. Spitting water from a vent is not, and a technician should look at it.
- Steam heat can be uneven from room to room. Radiators near the boiler tend to heat first.

## Hot-water boilers and baseboard

Many newer buildings and houses have a hot-water boiler. It heats water and pumps it through a closed loop of pipes to radiators or to fin-tube baseboard along the walls, then the water returns to the boiler. A hot-water system usually has a pressure and temperature gauge on the boiler and an expansion tank nearby that absorbs the extra volume as the water heats.

- Gurgling, trickling or rushing water sounds in a hot-water system usually mean air is trapped in the pipes or a radiator.
- A hot-water radiator that is cool at the top and warm at the bottom usually has air trapped in it.
- Ticking or creaking from baseboard as it heats and cools is common, because the metal expands and contracts.
- Common brands include Weil-McLain, Burnham, Navien, Peerless and Viessmann.

## Boilers in general

Many boilers run for 15 to 30 years with regular care. Boilers burn natural gas or oil, so they need a working vent and a carbon monoxide alarm nearby.

Water on the floor around a boiler is a leak and worth a technician visit. A boiler that shuts off and restarts by itself, or that lets the radiators cool off quickly, can point to a control problem.

Many New York City apartment buildings have a shared building boiler run by the building. If the whole building is cold, that is a question for the landlord or super first.

## Heat pumps and mini-splits for heating

A heat pump moves heat from one place to another instead of making it. In winter it pulls heat from outside air into the home. Modern cold-climate heat pumps keep working at low outdoor temperatures, though they make less heat as it gets colder.

A ductless mini-split is a heat pump with an outdoor unit linked by a thin line set to one or more indoor units on the wall or ceiling. Each indoor unit heats and cools its own room or zone. Common brands include Mitsubishi, Daikin and Fujitsu.

- A mini-split in heating mode will sometimes stop blowing for several minutes and show steam or water outside. That is the outdoor unit melting frost off its coil, called defrost, and it is normal.
- Air from a heat pump or mini-split often feels cooler than a furnace's, even when it is working correctly, because the air is warmed less.

## Electric baseboard heat

Electric baseboard heaters are metal strips along the wall that warm up when electricity runs through them. Each room usually has its own thermostat. They burn nothing, so there is no flue. A baseboard that smells like burning dust the first time it runs in the fall is often just dust burning off.

## New York City heat season

In New York City, the legal heat season for apartments runs from October 1 through May 31. During those months, landlords must keep apartments at least 68 degrees between 6 AM and 10 PM when it is below 55 degrees outside, and at least 62 degrees overnight. Tenants without heat can report it to 311.
