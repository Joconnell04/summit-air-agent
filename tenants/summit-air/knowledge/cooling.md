# Cooling equipment

## Central air conditioning

Central air has an outdoor unit, called the condenser, and an indoor coil that usually sits on top of the furnace or in an air handler. A blower sends cooled air through the same ducts used for heating. It cools the whole home on one thermostat. Common brands include Carrier, Trane, Lennox, Goodman and Rheem.

An air conditioner does not make cold; it moves heat from inside to outside using refrigerant, a fluid that cycles between the indoor coil and the outdoor unit. The refrigerant is sealed in, so it does not get used up like fuel. Low refrigerant means there is a leak.

## Ductless mini-splits for cooling

A mini-split cools room by room. An outdoor unit connects to one or more wall or ceiling units inside, and each indoor unit has its own remote or thermostat. Mini-splits are popular in New York City apartments and older houses where there is no room for ducts. Common brands include Mitsubishi, Daikin and Fujitsu.

- A blinking light on a mini-split indoor unit usually means the unit has detected a problem and stopped to protect itself. The pattern of the blinks, or a code on the display, tells a technician what it found, and the codes differ by brand. A steady filter light is only a reminder that the filter is due.
- Water dripping from the indoor unit usually points to a clogged or blocked drain line for the condensation.
- A mini-split that runs but doesn't cool well can have dirty filters, a dirty coil, or low refrigerant.

## Window and through-wall units

A window unit sits in a window opening and cools one room. A through-wall unit is built into a sleeve in the wall, which is common in New York City apartments. Both have a filter behind the front grille that should be kept clean.

Water dripping inside from a window unit often means it is not tilted slightly outward, or its drain is blocked. A unit that ices up usually has restricted airflow or low refrigerant.

## Common cooling symptoms

- An air conditioner that runs but the air isn't cold can come from a dirty filter, a dirty outdoor coil, low refrigerant, a thermostat set wrong, or a failing outdoor unit or compressor.
- Ice on the copper line or the indoor coil usually means poor airflow or low refrigerant. Running the system while it is frozen can damage the compressor.
- The outdoor unit not running while the indoor blower does can come from a tripped breaker, or a failed capacitor or contactor, which a technician checks.
- A central air conditioner that turns on and off every few minutes is short cycling, which can come from a dirty filter, low refrigerant, or a unit that is too large for the space.
- Warm or humid air from the vents while the system runs can mean the outdoor unit isn't doing its part.
- A musty smell when the air conditioner first starts can come from moisture in the coil or drain pan.

## Lifespan and tune-ups

Central air conditioners and heat pumps often last 10 to 15 years, depending on care and use. Mini-splits often last about the same. A spring tune-up checks the coils, refrigerant level, electrical connections and drain line before the hot weather.
