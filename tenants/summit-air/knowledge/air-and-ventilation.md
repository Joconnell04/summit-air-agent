# Ductwork, ventilation and indoor air

## Ductwork

Ducts are the metal or flexible tubes that carry heated or cooled air from a furnace or air handler to vents in each room. Supply ducts bring air out to the rooms and return ducts bring it back to the unit. Leaky, crushed or uninsulated ducts waste air and can leave some rooms hotter or colder than others. Ducts in unheated spaces such as attics and crawl spaces lose the most.

Many New York City apartments and older homes have no ducts at all because they are heated by radiators, which is why mini-splits are popular there.

## Air filters

A furnace or central-air filter catches dust before it reaches the blower and coil. A clogged filter cuts airflow, makes the system work harder and can cause uneven rooms or a frozen coil. Many filters are changed every one to three months; a thin one in a home with pets or heavy dust may need it sooner, and a thicker one may last longer.

A filter's MERV number rates how fine a particle it catches. A higher number catches smaller particles but also restricts airflow more, so not every system can take a very high rating. A technician can say what a particular system handles.

Mini-splits and window units have their own washable filters behind the front panel.

## Bathroom and kitchen exhaust

Exhaust fans pull moist or smelly air out of a bathroom or kitchen and send it outside through a duct. A bathroom fan that is weak, noisy or that leaves the mirror fogged for a long time may have a blocked duct or a worn motor. A range hood that is not vented outside only filters grease and recirculates the air.

## ERV and HRV ventilators

Tight, modern homes need a way to bring in fresh air. An HRV, a heat recovery ventilator, pulls stale indoor air out and fresh outdoor air in, and passes the heat from the outgoing air to the incoming air so less heating energy is lost. An ERV, an energy recovery ventilator, does the same and also moves some moisture between the two air streams. ERVs are more common in places with humid summers, and HRVs in colder, drier places. The two airstreams never mix.

## Humidifiers and dehumidifiers

Heated air in winter is dry, and a whole-house humidifier attached to the furnace adds moisture to the air going through the ducts. Dry air can mean static shocks, dry skin and cracking wood. Too much moisture causes condensation on windows and mold, so comfortable indoor humidity is generally in the middle range, about 30 to 50 percent.

A humidifier has a water supply line and a pad or drum that needs regular cleaning or replacement. Dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air in damp basements and humid summers; a central air conditioner also removes some humidity as it cools.

## Air cleaners

A portable air cleaner with a HEPA filter cleans a single room. Whole-house air cleaners are installed in the return duct. Ozone-producing air cleaners are not recommended.
