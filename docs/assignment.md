The Scenario
You’re a Forward Deployed Engineer at Revin. A new customer, Summit Air, a residential HVAC company in NYC, has asked you to build an AI phone agent that handles their inbound calls.

Here’s what they told you on the kickoff call:
“We’re a 40-tech, residential-only shop across three counties, and our phones ring off the hook, especially when the first heat wave or cold snap hits. Most calls are people whose AC went out, furnace won’t kick on, or they need their annual maintenance scheduled. We want an AI agent that answers the phone, figures out what they need, and books a service call. If someone’s got no heat in January with an elderly person in the house, that needs to get flagged as a priority immediately.”

Your job: build the agent for Summit Air.
The agent should be able to:

* Answer a call and figure out what HVAC issue the caller has
* Determine if the caller is residential or commercial
* Determine if the caller is already a member
* Collect all the information a technician needs to run the appointment
* Distinguish routine maintenance from urgent situations (no heat in winter, no AC with a medical condition or elderly resident, gas smell)
* Book the appointment or confirm next steps with the caller


There are no constraints on tools, frameworks, or models. Use whatever you’d use if a real customer needed this tomorrow.

What we need from you 24 hours before your session:

* A phone number where we can call and test your agent live
* Access to your repo (please share via email with me and the Revin team) so we can review your prompting and architecture ahead of time


Your session (45 minutes)
You’ll meet with [Paul Appleby](https://www.linkedin.com/in/paul-appleby/), one of our Forward Deployed Engineers. After a quick intro, we will treat this as a real customer demo. Paul will play the owner of Summit Air, seeing their new agent for the first time.

You’ll lead the meeting from start to finish, the same way you would with a real customer:

* Run the demo. Open the meeting, walk Summit Air through what you built and how it meets their requirements, and call the agent live.
* Handle the customer. Expect the kinds of questions, concerns, and pushback a real business owner would raise. Answer them the way you would in the field.
* Debrief honestly. After the live calls, tell Summit Air how the calls went, what worked, what didn’t, and what you’d improve before going live.
* Close with next steps. Propose what a rollout would look like from here.


For the last part of the session, Paul will step out of character. Be ready to dig into prompting, architecture, and the tradeoffs you made, and you should be ready to debug an issues live.

What we’re evaluating

* The agent: Does it work, does it meet Summit Air’s requirements, and how does it handle the unexpected?
* Conversation quality: Does it feel like a good experience for the caller, or like a robot reading a form?
* How you run the room: Can you lead a customer meeting, explain technical decisions in business terms, handle tough questions, and set honest expectations?
* Judgment: What you chose to build vs. skip given the time constraint, and how you explain those choices to a customer.
